More Details

Gridfinity tray with quarter increments

OOBB shape: gridfinity_tray_quarter_increment.

Uses the existing Gridfinity Extended tray and pad profiles, with a separate bottom mask that splits fractional feet. The original gridfinity_tray_raw component and its vendored sources are unchanged.

Width is X, height is Y, and depth is Z, all in Gridfinity units (42/42/7 mm). Both gridfinity_width/gridfinity_height/gridfinity_depth and the shorter width/height/depth aliases work. Position and rotation work as usual.

Tray footprintBottom feet
0.75 × 10.5 × 1 + 0.25 × 1
1 × 0.751 × 0.5 + 1 × 0.25
0.75 × 0.50.5 × 0.5 + 0.25 × 0.5
0.5 × 0.250.5 × 0.25
1.25 × 11 × 1 + 0.25 × 1
1.75 × 11 × 1 + 0.5 × 1 + 0.25 × 1

Only one axis may end in .25 or .75. The other must be a multiple of 0.5. Dimensions must be positive multiples of 0.25; unsupported dimensions raise an error. Whole-unit feet come first, then any half-unit and quarter-unit remainder. The near alignment places the remainder at the far end; far mirrors it. Center alignment is unsupported. sub_pitch and flat_base are retained in the common parameter signature but the component controls them to preserve this foot layout and the standard floor height.

The interior now follows the bottom feet by default, like the default tray's efficient floor. Each whole/half/quarter foot has its own recess, with material left across the seams to keep the tray closed. The nominal 0.7 mm floor has an additional 0.01 mm boolean clearance. Use efficient_floor="off" to retain a flat interior floor.

oobb.append_full(
    thing,
    shape="gridfinity_tray_quarter_increment",
    gridfinity_width=0.75,
    gridfinity_height=1,
    gridfinity_depth=18 / 7,
    wall_thickness=1.25,
    lip_style="none",
)

Validation: python -m pytest -q tests/test_gridfinity_tray_quarter_increment.py. The geometry tests require OpenSCAD with the Manifold backend, trimesh and numpy. They check individual foot dimensions, one connected watertight mesh, closed foot bottoms, and the depth of the recesses, with both efficient and flat floors. Physical fit still requires a print check.
